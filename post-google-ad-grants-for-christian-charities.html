Google Ad Grants for Christian charities: what you need to know

Up to $10,000 a month in free advertising sits unused, or poorly used, in more charity accounts than you'd think. Here's how it actually works, and how to make it count.

Google Ad Grants Charities
Asif Valiji3 December 20256 min read

The Google Ad Grant gives eligible registered charities up to $10,000 (around £8,000) of free Google Ads spend every month. For a Christian charity running education programmes, selling products or tools, or promoting an event, that's a meaningful budget — provided it's set up and managed properly. A surprising number of eligible charities either haven't applied, or applied once and let the account drift into non-compliance.

Who's eligible

Most UK-registered charities qualify. You'll need a valid charity registration, a working website that meets Google's content and security requirements, and someone willing to keep the account within Google's rules once it's approved. The application itself isn't especially difficult — the harder part, in our experience, is what comes after approval.

Why grant accounts get suspended

The Google Ad Grant comes with stricter rules than a standard paid account: minimum click-through rate thresholds, required use of specific campaign types, geo-targeting requirements, and a ban on single-word or overly generic keywords. Accounts that were set up in a hurry, or left unmanaged after launch, tend to fall foul of one of these within a few months — and a suspended grant is a slower, more frustrating process to reinstate than to have set up properly the first time.

The grant itself is the easy part. Keeping it running is where the real work is.

Making the budget count

A well-run Ad Grant account is built around the same discipline as any paid search account, just within tighter rules: keyword research tied to your charity's actual education, products, services and events; tightly themed ad groups rather than broad ones; and landing pages that answer the exact question someone searched. The charities that get the most from their grant treat it as a genuine campaign, not a free top-up to whatever they were already doing.

Where SEO fits alongside it

The Ad Grant works best paired with organic content, not instead of it. Paid traffic gets you visibility immediately; SEO content built around the same keyword research keeps working long after a campaign ends, and gives the Ad Grant campaigns better landing pages to send people to. Charities that invest in both tend to rely on paid spend less over time, not more.
