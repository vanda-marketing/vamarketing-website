Why charities are different

A donor isn't a customer

Retail PPC and SEO are built to shorten the distance between a search and a sale. Charity marketing has to do something harder first: build enough trust and understanding that someone is willing to give, with nothing coming back to them directly. We've spent four years specialising in exactly that distinction.

How we work with charities

A process built for nonprofit budgets and boards

  1. Audit & eligibility check

    We review your current digital presence and confirm Google Ad Grant eligibility if you're not already enrolled.

  2. Keyword & cause research

    We find the specific, lower-competition search terms tied to your actual programmes — where a major national charity isn't already outbidding you.

    Plain-English monthly reporting tied to donor engagement and cost-per-supporter, not just clicks and impressions.

FAQ

Common questions from charities

What does digital marketing for charities actually involve?

For most charities it means three things working together: PPC campaigns (often via the Google Ad Grant), SEO content that ranks for the causes and services the charity provides, and donor-focused messaging that educates before it asks for a donation.

What is the Google Ad Grant, and can any charity use it?

It gives eligible registered charities up to $10,000 (around £8,000) of free monthly Google Ads spend. Most UK-registered charities qualify, though the account needs managing to Google's compliance rules or the grant can be suspended.

How much does charity digital marketing cost?

We manage charity PPC accounts at a reduced rate of £36 per hour. Ad spend itself is often covered in full or in part by the Google Ad Grant.

How is marketing a charity different from marketing a business?

A donor isn't a customer. Campaigns that explain the cause and its impact first consistently outperform a direct "donate now" ask.
