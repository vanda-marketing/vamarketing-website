Elevating a fine jeweller

How two years of paid and organic search data reshaped a specialist workshop's entire website — and the results that followed.

Case Study SEO
Asif Valiji31 October 20256 min read

The challenge

The client is a fine jewellery workshop doing genuinely excellent work — bespoke commissions, heirloom restoration, platinum and precious-metal repair — based in an affluent area with no shortage of quality competitors nearby. The craftsmanship was never the problem. Being found by the right client, and getting them through the workshop door rather than a competitor's, was.

Three things needed solving at once: differentiating from other quality jewellers in the same postcode, communicating the workshop's specific expertise to a clientele that expects precision and discretion, and converting online interest into physical footfall — since so much of the value in a purchase like this comes from seeing and handling the piece in person.

Starting from what people actually search

The turning point was realising that prospective clients weren't searching "jeweller near me" — they were searching for specific, often quite technical solutions: "platinum ring repair," "heirloom remodelling," "resizing an antique band without damaging the setting." Generic service pages couldn't answer those searches with any authority.

So the site was restructured around search intent rather than around the business's own internal service list:

  • Dedicated pages for each specific service, each with real process detail and portfolio photography of comparable work
  • Paid campaigns built to connect a specific search directly to the matching landing page, rather than to a general homepage

That direct relevance did something a generic ad or page never could: it built instant confidence that this workshop had done exactly this kind of work before.

The traffic was never the goal. Getting the right specialist search to land on proof of the exact right expertise was.

Letting two years of data redesign the site

Rather than treating the initial site as finished, we kept it as a live source of evidence. Two years of paid and organic performance data showed exactly which services, pages and phrasing were converting, and which weren't pulling their weight. That data became the brief for a full site redesign — not a guess at what might look better, but a rebuild informed by what had actually been proven to work, alongside the technical fundamentals (page speed, Core Web Vitals) that a growing, ageing site had started to lose.

The result

The combined approach — search-intent content, tightly matched paid campaigns, and a redesign built on real behavioural data rather than instinct — delivered a triple-digit improvement in performance. The underlying lesson has stuck with us since: paid advertising data isn't separate from design decisions. Treated as intelligence rather than just a spend report, it's one of the best briefs a redesign can have.
