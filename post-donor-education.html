Donor education: why explaining wins more support than asking

Nearly two decades in digital marketing has taught us the same lesson from both the commercial and charity side: people give their attention, and their support, to whoever explains the problem best — not whoever asks the loudest.

SEO Donor Engagement
Asif Valiji26 November 20257 min read

Search rankings, impressions and clicks are useful measures, but none of them are the point when you're working with a Christian charity. The point is whether the right supporter, searching for the right thing — a cause, a course, a resource, an event — lands somewhere that genuinely answers their question. That's a precision problem, not a volume one.

A donor isn't a customer

A donor decides to give with nothing coming back to them directly. That's a different decision to a purchase, and it needs different groundwork. A donor starts from a genuine interest in a cause or a need, and searches around that — not around a charity's name or slogan. They build trust from whoever's content actually answers what they were looking for, long before they consider giving anything.

Charities that can't articulate their specific work clearly, in language that matches how a supporter actually searches, rarely get found at all. It doesn't matter how good the underlying work is. If a website talks about the charity in general terms, a searching donor moves on before they've even seen it.

The mistake many charities make

The common error isn't a lack of effort — it's promoting the charity from the inside out. Charities write about their mission in the language they use internally, without first mapping that against what a supporter actually types into a search bar when they have a genuine interest or need. The result reads well to the charity and means very little to the person standing in front of it.

The strategy: educate before you ask

The fix isn't a louder donation ask. It's the opposite: content that explains the cause, the education, the product or the event clearly, written so a supporter immediately recognises what they were looking for in it. The less a charity leads with asking, the more it's educating — and it's the educating that builds the trust an ask alone skips past.

A donor doesn't want to be asked before they understand what they'd be supporting. Show the need first.

Why this matters more as attention gets harder to earn

Traffic and advertising costs both keep climbing, which makes the quality of who arrives at your site matter more than how many people do. Educational content does a kind of filtering an ask alone can't: it attracts people already engaged with the specific cause, product or event, and it quietly moves on the people who were never going to support it anyway. Fewer visitors, better relationships.

If any of this sounds like your charity — a genuine cause or offering that isn't showing up where supporters are actually searching — that's usually the first thing worth fixing, well before ad spend or volume.
