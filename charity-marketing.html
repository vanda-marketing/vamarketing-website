The basics

What is charity marketing?

Charity marketing is the work of helping people discover your charity, understand what it does, trust it and take part. That might mean donating, subscribing, volunteering, joining a course or coming to an event. Done well, it is less about broadcasting and more about building relationships with people who are already looking for what you offer.

It sits alongside fundraising rather than replacing it. Marketing creates the awareness, understanding and trust that make people want to give; fundraising invites them to do so. A charity with strong marketing finds fundraising easier, because supporters already know and believe in the work.

Why it is different

Charity marketing is not commercial marketing with a softer tone

Trust comes first

Supporters give because they believe in your cause. Every message has to be honest, clear and consistent with why you exist.

Budgets are limited

Most charities cannot buy their way to visibility, so research, good content and the Google Ad Grant matter far more.

Many goals at once

One charity may need donors, volunteers, students, members and event attendees. Each group searches differently.

Supporters are a community

The people who back your work are not an audience; they are the body. The aim is to move them from hearing about you to speaking for you.

Questions

Charity marketing FAQs

What is charity marketing?

Charity marketing is the work of helping people discover your charity, understand what it does, trust it and take part, whether that means donating, subscribing, volunteering, joining a course or coming to an event. Done well, it focuses on building long-term relationships with the people who are already looking for what you offer.

How is charity marketing different from fundraising?

Fundraising asks for support. Charity marketing creates the awareness, understanding and trust that make people want to give in the first place. The two work together: marketing brings the right people to your charity and keeps them engaged, and fundraising invites them to give.

Can charities use Google Ads and SEO?

Yes. Eligible registered charities can apply for the Google Ad Grant, which provides up to $10,000 (around £8,000) a month in free Google advertising. SEO, meaning content written around what your supporters are genuinely searching for, builds visibility over time and reduces reliance on paid spend. We use both, alongside targeted advertising.

How much should a charity spend on marketing?

There is no single right figure, because it depends on your goals, your audience and what you already have in place. We start from what you want to achieve, then plan the hours and activity needed to reach it. Our charity rates are £30 per hour for charities with income under £1m, £36 per hour from £1m to £3m and £50 per hour over £3m.

How long does charity marketing take to work?

Paid search can bring visitors quickly once it is set up properly, while content and SEO build momentum over months. Our average working period with charities is 12 to 24 months, because lasting results come from steady, well-researched work rather than a single campaign.

Do you only work with Christian charities?

Charity marketing for Christian charities is our main focus. We combine nearly 20 years of digital marketing experience with a commitment to serving Christian charities, particularly in education and development, and in the products, services and events that support their growth.
