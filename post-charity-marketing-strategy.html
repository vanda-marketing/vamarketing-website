Charity marketing strategy: a practical six-step guide

How to plan charity marketing that brings the right supporters to you, in plain steps you can start this month.

Charity marketing Strategy
Asif Valiji26 October 20266 min read

A good charity marketing strategy starts with one clear goal, a real understanding of the people you want to reach, and content that answers the questions they are already asking. Everything else, from adverts to email, builds on that. Here are the six steps we work through with the charities we support.

1. Start with one goal

Charities often want everything at once: more donors, more volunteers, more course places, more event tickets. Pick the one that matters most this year and write it down in plain terms, such as "fill our autumn course" or "double our email subscribers". A single goal tells you which audience, which message and which channel to start with.

2. Understand who you are trying to reach

Describe the person you want to connect with: what they are worried about, what they hope for, and what would make them trust you. Their psychology matters more than their demographics. A head teacher, a church leader and a donor may all care about your cause, but they ask very different questions and respond to different things.

3. Research what they actually search for

People rarely search for a charity by name until they already know it. They search for a problem or a question. Keyword research shows the natural phrases they use, how often, and who else is answering. This is the foundation of search-led charity marketing, and it is where most wasted effort can be avoided.

4. Build content that answers those questions

Turn what you found into articles, guides, videos or podcasts that answer real questions honestly. Include real stories with names and context, and give every page a clear next step, whether that is to subscribe, enquire, volunteer or give. Content that genuinely helps is what search engines, and increasingly AI answer engines, choose to show.

5. Add paid reach carefully

Once the content is working, paid search can put it in front of more people. Eligible charities can apply for the Google Ad Grant, worth up to $10,000 (around £8,000) a month in free advertising. It works best when there is strong content for the adverts to land on, which is why we usually build content first. Our guide to PPC for charities explains how to avoid the common mistakes.

6. Measure what matters, then keep going

Track the outcomes that match your goal, such as new subscribers, enquiries or bookings, rather than visits alone. Review monthly, do more of what works and stop what does not. Charity marketing compounds: our average working period with charities is 12 to 24 months, because steady, well-researched work builds momentum that one-off campaigns cannot.

Where to begin

If you only do one thing this week, write down your single goal and the one person you most want to reach. If you would like a second opinion, our charity marketing team is happy to talk it through, and you can see how we work on our process page.
