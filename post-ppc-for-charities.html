PPC for charities: a guide to effective ad management

The nonprofit sector doesn't convert the way retail does. Here's what we've learned running paid search for charities.

PPC Charities
Asif Valiji28 November 20256 min read

We've spent the last four years running paid search specifically for charities, and the lesson that took longest to learn is this: a donation ask is not a product pitch. Someone deciding whether to buy a kettle and someone deciding whether to support a cause are doing very different things psychologically, and an ad written like the first will underperform badly with the second.

Why the direct ask struggles

The instinct with charity PPC is often to lead with the ask — "Donate today," "Give £10 now." It's the most literal translation of the goal into an ad. It's also competing against every other charity doing the same thing, in a landscape that's grown considerably more crowded and more skeptical since 2020. Donors have learned to scroll past a direct ask before they've had any reason to trust the organisation behind it.

What tends to work better is treating the ad as the first step of a relationship, not the whole of it — leading with what the charity does and why it matters, and letting the donation ask come after that groundwork, not instead of it.

Educate first, ask second

Campaigns that pair PPC with a short video or a well-written article — something that actually explains the mission, who it helps, and what a donation changes — consistently convert better than campaigns that go straight for the ask. It costs a little more patience to set up. It costs a lot less in wasted spend once it's running, because the people who do convert already understand what they're supporting.

Capturing attention isn't the hard part in this sector. Capturing hearts and minds is.

Keyword strategy: go where the big charities aren't

The obvious keywords — broad terms tied to well-known causes — are expensive precisely because every major charity is bidding on them too. Smaller organisations rarely win that auction, and even when they do, the traffic is broad rather than genuinely interested.

Long-tail, specific terms are usually the better investment: the particular programme, the particular community, the particular problem the charity solves that a big-name competitor doesn't. Lower competition, lower cost, and the person searching is closer to actually caring about the answer.

Where the Google Ad Grant fits

Many charities are sitting on a Google Ad Grant they're either not using or not using well — free monthly ad spend that comes with its own rules and quality thresholds. Getting real value from it usually means the same discipline as any paid account: tight ad groups, honest landing pages, and campaigns structured around what the charity can actually deliver, not just what sounds compelling in an ad.

None of this is complicated once it's set up properly. It just needs treating as its own discipline, not a smaller version of retail PPC. We manage charity accounts at a reduced £36/hour, because getting this right for a nonprofit budget matters as much as getting it right for a commercial one.
