Digital marketing for cladding companies

Why PPC on its own tends to under-perform for specialist contractors — and what to run alongside it instead.

PPC SEO
V&A Author30 January5 min read

Cladding is not a generic trade, and it doesn't market like one. A contractor might specialise in rainscreen systems, fire-rated panels, or heritage rendering, and sell almost entirely into a handful of postcodes. Generic visibility — ranking for "cladding company" nationally — rarely turns into a job. What turns into a job is being found by the right specifier, in the right region, at the exact moment they're comparing suppliers.

The problem with PPC on its own

Paid search is the fastest way to appear in front of that person, which is exactly why so many cladding contractors lean on it entirely. But run in isolation, PPC tends to produce a familiar pattern: plenty of clicks, a respectable cost-per-click, and a thin trickle of enquiries that go quiet the moment you ask about scope, budget or timeline. The ad got the click. It didn't do the job of proving competence.

That's a structural issue, not a targeting mistake. An ad has a headline and two lines of description — nowhere near enough room to show a specifier that you've handled their exact system before, on a building like theirs, to the standard their project needs.

What actually builds confidence before the call

Architects, main contractors and building owners researching cladding suppliers do a version of due diligence before they'll invite a quote. They want to see the relevant project type, the certifications, and evidence the work has been done before — not just claimed. That's content PPC can point to, but can't replace:

  • Project pages built around specific systems and applications, not a generic "our services" page
  • Photography and short write-ups of completed jobs similar to the one being specified
  • Clear, honest information about certifications, fire ratings and compliance — the things a specifier is actually screening for

Paid campaigns get people to that content quickly. SEO keeps bringing people to it for free, for as long as the content stays relevant.

Where AI-assisted search fits in

A newer shift is worth planning for now rather than later: specifiers and buyers increasingly start their research inside tools like ChatGPT or Gemini rather than a search bar — asking for contractors who've handled a specific system in a specific region, and getting a short, synthesised answer back. Being one of the businesses those tools can confidently describe depends on the same foundation as good SEO: clear, specific, well-structured information about what you actually do and where you've done it. Thin, generic web copy doesn't get surfaced. Detailed, evidenced project content does.

The goal isn't more traffic. It's fewer, better-qualified conversations with people who already believe you can do the job.

Putting it together

None of this argues against PPC — it's still the quickest lever to pull when you need enquiries this month. The point is what it should be paired with: search-optimised project pages that do the convincing an ad can't, built with the specific systems, regions and certifications your ideal client is actually searching for. That combination is what turns clicks into shortlists, and shortlists into instructed jobs.
